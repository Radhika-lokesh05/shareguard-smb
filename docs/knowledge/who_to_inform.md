# Playbook: Who to Inform Following a Ransomware Incident

A ransomware incident involves technical, operational, and regulatory responsibilities. Use this guide to notify the appropriate parties in an organized, timely manner.

---

## 1. Internal Key Decision Makers
- **Notify leadership immediately**: Inform your managing director, office partners, or executive team.
- **Establish an out-of-band communication channel**: Use a secondary method (such as phone calls or approved secure messaging apps) in case your office email system is compromised.
- **Appoint a single point of contact**: Designate one person to liaise with external technical partners and insurers to keep communications consistent.

## 2. Your IT Support Provider / Managed Service Provider (MSP)
- **Call your emergency support line**: Notify your IT provider that an active ransomware incident has been detected on Samba shares.
- **Provide ShareGuard alert telemetry**: Share the timestamp, suspect client IP, affected user account, and triggered detection rules.
- **Request specialist incident support**: Ask whether they have dedicated cybersecurity or incident response personnel on call.

## 3. Your Cyber Insurance Carrier
- **Call the insurance claims hotline promptly**: Many cyber insurance policies require immediate or early notification as a condition of claim coverage.
- **Engage approved vendors**: Insurance carriers often provide access to pre-vetted forensic investigators, legal specialists, and recovery consultants covered under your policy terms.
- **Document all actions**: Keep a written log of timestamps, calls made, and guidance received for insurance record-keeping.

## 4. Legal Counsel and Regulatory Advisors
- **Consult qualified legal counsel**: Before notifying clients or the public, seek advice from a specialized privacy or data breach attorney.
- **Understand notification obligations**:
  - Many jurisdictions and industries impose mandatory breach-notification deadlines when customer, patient, or employee data is accessed or encrypted.
  - These statutory reporting requirements differ significantly by country, state, and business sector (such as healthcare, finance, or retail).
  - *Important Notice*: Because legal requirements and notification windows vary widely by location and the nature of compromised data, you must consult local law and your legal advisor to determine the specific legal obligations that apply to your business. Do not assume or rely on generic timelines.

## 5. Law Enforcement and National Cyber Agencies
- **Report to national cyber authorities**: Most countries operate cyber reporting portals (such as CISA/IC3 in the United States, NCSC in the United Kingdom, or ACSC in Australia) to track ransomware campaigns.
- **Do not share sensitive passwords**: Provide incident facts without sharing confidential employee credentials or private customer records.
